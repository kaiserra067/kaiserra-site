What we build

  • Ra Strategy

    In development

    Europe, 1 January 1936. A few short years before the continent goes up in flames, you take charge of a nation and decide how ready it will be. Ra Strategy is a real-time grand strategy game: pause to plan, or let the days race by at up to 5x.

    • Any flag you like. Every country on the map is playable, from the great powers to the small states caught between them.
    • Build the war machine. Raise military and civilian factories, dockyards and resource plants city by city, then run production lines that get sharper the longer they run.
    • NewBalance the books. Set income and corporate taxes, pay for consumer goods and factory upkeep, and take out loans when the war can't wait.
    • Steel decides wars. Oil, steel, aluminium, tungsten, chromium and coal. Mine them at home or buy them on a global market, because every shortage slows your factories.
    • Race through the tech tree. Land, air, naval, industry and electronics, from Great War rifles to the heavy tanks of 1942.
    • NewNo idle labs. Research points arrive every day, and a branch left without a project throws them away.
    • NewChoose what to build. Only a handful of construction projects run at once, so every new factory is a decision.
    • Talk before you shoot. Justify war goals, trade military access, guarantee independence, sign non-aggression pacts or cut a rival off with an embargo.
    • Politics at home. Parties rise and fall, and every law, from conscription to propaganda, has a price.
    • NewReform has a cost. Changing a law spends diplomatic power, so every reform has to be worth it.
    • Read the map. Switch between political, resource and terrain views of a Europe split into provinces, each with its own cities and manpower.
    • NewHolding is not belonging. Every province tracks who controls it and whose people live there.
    • Built on balance. Every formula is tested on its own before it reaches the game. Single-player scenarios and multiplayer are planned.

How we work

Rules first. Every system starts as a plain description of how it should behave: how fast a camp produces iron, how much food a unit eats, what a mod should change.

AI writes the code. We hand the description to AI, run what comes back, and iterate until it behaves.

Math gets checked. Strategy games live or die on their numbers. We test the formulas on their own before they go anywhere near a player.

Small and fast. A small team with AI can try more ideas in a week than a large one can in a month, and drop the ones that aren't fun.
